Read the caveats before quoting any of this. The benchmark, its weights and five of the six drivers were written by the developer of one of the contestants. That is stated in full below, along with the three figures that are artifacts of how something is measured rather than results.

Round one · Greater Diplomacy 5’s 1939

906 provinces, 35 to 37 AI players. GD5’s own historical scenario, converted for Open Doctrines with Dragoman. Freeciv and Unciv cannot load either map, so they are sized to match.

Round two · Open Doctrines’ 1939

1,298 land provinces, 63 AI players — more than the 34 major civilizations Unciv’s ruleset has, so 29 of its players are city-states. FreeOrion’s run here is not published; see the caveats.

This round is a stress test for Greater Diplomacy 5, not its normal case. Open Doctrines’ map keeps its 8192 × 4096 picture, about 25 times the pixels of GD5’s own 1939. GD5 redraws its map layers from that picture every turn, so its 4.1 turns a minute here mostly measures the picture size. Read its real performance from round one, where it managed 74.

Who wrote it

OJH, its score weights, and the Freeciv, Unciv, FreeOrion, FreeCol and network drivers were all written by the developer of Open Doctrines. That is a conflict of interest, and it is stated here rather than buried. Greater Diplomacy 5 is measured through a hook offered to its own developer as a pull request; until that is merged, OJH runs GD5 from that branch.

Open Doctrines' network bytes are reported, not captured

Joining an Open Doctrines match needs a signed-in account, so no client can sit on the wire. The game reports the turn delta its host broadcasts, times the number of clients. Unciv's figure is its synced save after each turn. Freeciv's and GD5's are bytes actually on the wire through OJH's counting relay. Those are not the same kind of measurement, and the first is the one that flatters Open Doctrines.

Freeciv's 0.1 ms turn delivery is an artifact, not a result

Delivery is timed from a turn mark to the end of the first burst of data after it. Freeciv writes its turn log line after it has already sent most of the turn, so the burst OJH sees is a small trailing packet. It earns Freeciv its only first place, and it counts for 4% of Freeciv's score.

Frame rates are each game's own renderer, at its own resolution

Open Doctrines and Unciv draw with OpenGL at 1600 × 900. Greater Diplomacy 5 draws in software, with pygame, at a fixed 1280 × 720. GD5's 1% low comes from the frames drawn while a turn resolves on its background thread; Open Doctrines resolves a turn inside one frame behind a loading screen, so it has no equivalent scene at all. Freeciv has no frame rate to measure: its client redraws only when the map changes.

Two scores are partial, and one is provisional

FreeOrion and FreeCol were measured for turn speed only — 67% of the weight — because network and frame rate would need their clients driven, which OJH does not do yet. Their scores leave out the parts where the other games gain and lose points. Freeciv's is provisional for the frame-rate reason above.

The second round is a stress test for Greater Diplomacy 5, not its normal case

Open Doctrines' 1939 keeps its 8192 × 4096 map picture, about 25 times the pixels of GD5's own 1939 at 1881 × 698. GD5 redraws its map layers from that picture every turn, so its turn time in that round mostly measures the picture size. GD5's real performance is the first round's.

FreeCol does not play out identically twice

Three runs on the same seed on GD5's 1939 spread from 39.8 to 57.2 turns per minute. The median is shown. On Open Doctrines' 1939 it ran once.

FreeOrion is measured exactly as it ships

Debug logging included: the server and each AI client write tens of megabytes of logs a minute, and --log-level did not change that. Its run on Open Doctrines' 1939 is not published at all — the Mac slept for 66 minutes during turns 110–112, so that run's turns per minute measures the sleep. It will be rerun.

Regions are not the same unit in every game

Open Doctrines and Greater Diplomacy 5 count provinces. Freeciv and Unciv count tiles. FreeOrion counts star systems. Region-throughput figures are therefore comparable within a game and indicative between games, which is why turn and player throughput carry more of the score than world throughput does.

The CPU reference score is OJH’s own fixed workload, timed on this machine. Divide a result by it to compare across hardware.

250 turns, every player run by the game’s own AI. Turn speed is the median of three runs on GD5’s 1939 and one run on Open Doctrines’ 1939. Network runs are 20 turns with two clients. Each frame-rate scene is timed for five seconds after settling, with the late-game map measured after 20 turns. Start-up and world generation are timed separately and are not part of the turns.

Player-turns and region-turns multiply turn speed by the size of the game, so a larger world is not punished for being larger.
